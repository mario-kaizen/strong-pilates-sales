### Track 1: Intro Offer Funnel (Weeks 1 to 8)

Presented as a card/panel with icon.

A persistent campaign running from day one through to end of June and beyond. This is the engine that keeps feeding the pipeline.

- **Funnel over lead forms.** A dedicated landing page that showcases the Geelong studio, the instructors, the experience, and social proof from real members. Not a two-field form that collects a name and phone number with zero context.

- **Proper nurture.** When someone submits, they don't just get a generic "thanks for signing up" SMS. They get a sequence that builds trust, shows them what to expect, and connects them with a real person at the studio before their first session.
- **CRM pipeline visibility.** Every lead tracked from ad click to intro offer purchase to membership conversion. No more guessing who's where.

### Track 2: Open Day Event (Weeks 6 to 8)

Presented as a card/panel with icon.

The big moment. End of June. This is where the database you've been building gets activated in one concentrated push.

- **Sell before, during, after.** Anyone who purchases in the lead-up counts. On the day, a membership offer for attendees. After the event, a follow-up sequence for everyone who came but didn't convert.
- **A face for the brand.** Your business partner is in-studio full-time from mid-June. For the first time, there's someone on the ground every day to lead the push.

> "Track 1 fills the database. Track 2 converts it. Neither works alone. Without the funnel, you don't have enough people to invite. Without the event, the leads sit in the CRM and decay. Together, they create a moment that generates momentum."

## Section 4: The Funnel

**Label:** 04 — The Funnel
**Title:** Why a Funnel, Not a Lead Form
**Subtitle:** The difference between collecting names and building trust before someone walks through the door.

### The Problem with Lead Forms

Presented as a two-column comparison card.

**Lead Form (what you're running now):**
- Two fields: name + phone number
- No context about who you are or what the experience is like
- Person fills it out and forgets within 24 hours
- Sales team calls a cold lead who barely remembers submitting
- CPL looks good on paper ($19.80) but cost per actual membership is high
- The database grows but the conversion rate stays low

**Funnel (what we're building):**
- Dedicated landing page showcasing STRONG Geelong specifically
- Studio imagery, instructor profiles, member testimonials
- The offer explained clearly with what to expect at your first session
- When they submit, they've already seen who you are and want to come in
- Nurture sequence continues building trust automatically
- By the time your team calls, the person is warm — not cold

### What the Funnel Includes

Presented as a visual flow (top to bottom):

1. **Hero** — Studio imagery, headline that speaks to the avatar, clear offer (e.g. "5 sessions for $50")
2. **Social Proof** — Real member quotes, before/after experiences, "why I chose STRONG"
3. **What to Expect** — The STRONG experience explained: reformer Pilates, strength, cardio, the vibe
4. **The Offer** — Clear pricing, what's included, no ambiguity
5. **Inquiry Form** — Name, phone, email, preferred class time
6. **Thank-You Page** — Next steps, booking link, what happens now
7. **Mobile-first** — 90%+ of traffic comes from Meta ads on mobile

### How It Connects to Your Sales Pipeline

Visual flow diagram:

```
Ad → Funnel → Form Submission → Grow CRM (tagged by campaign + source)
                                    ↓
                            Nurture Sequence fires
                            (SMS Day 0, Email Day 1, SMS Day 3)
                                    ↓
                            Sales team picks up
                            (Call → Book first 2 sessions → Follow-up at session 3-4)
                                    ↓
                            Intro offer → Membership conversion
```

### Annotation

> "Your sales team already has a great process: call immediately, book the first two sessions, follow up at session three. The funnel doesn't replace that — it feeds it with warmer leads who already know who you are."

## Section 6: Content Direction

**Label:** 06 — Content
**Title:** What to Post and Why It Matters
**Subtitle:** A content playbook specific enough for your social media team to execute — starting this week.

### The Problem

> "Your social media is currently full of trendy reels that get views but don't build trust. 4,000 views on a Beyonce reel is meaningless if nobody knows who you are, what the studio looks like, or why they should walk through the door. Views without context is the same problem as leads without conversion."

### Content Types (Priority Order)

Presented as numbered cards with icons:

**1. Social Proof Reels (2 per week minimum)**
- Member spotlight: 15-30 seconds. Real member talking about their experience.
- Format: Instructor introduces the member, member shares one thing they love.
- Example: "This is Sarah, she started 3 months ago and couldn't hold a plank. Now she's in our advanced class."
- Why: This is the single most missing piece. People need to see real people like them inside the studio.

**2. Studio Experience Reels (2 per week minimum)**
- Show the workout. The reformers, the energy, the class atmosphere.
- Not a tutorial. Not a trendy transition. Just authentic footage of what it feels like to be in a STRONG Geelong class.
- Capture the sounds: the music, the instructor cueing, the machines moving.
- Mix class types: morning energy vs evening grind. Different instructors.

**3. Instructor Personality (1 per week)**
- Short clips of instructors being themselves. Coaching cues, encouragement, post-class banter.
- Makes the studio human. People don't join brands — they join people.

**4. Behind the Scenes (1 per week as needed)**
- Setting up for class, cleaning the reformers, the team chatting before doors open.
- Shows professionalism and care. Builds trust that the studio is well-run.

### What NOT to Post

- Trendy lip-sync or dance reels
- Memes that don't feature the studio
- Generic fitness quotes on stock imagery
- Anything that could be posted by any studio anywhere
- Content with no face in it

### Content Calendar (Weekly Cadence)

| Day | Content Type | Owner |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| Monday | Social proof reel (member spotlight) | Social media team |
| Tuesday | Studio experience reel (class footage) | Social media team |
| Wednesday | Rest or behind-the-scenes | Social media team |
| Thursday | Social proof reel (second member spotlight) | Social media team |
| Friday | Studio experience or instructor personality | Social media team |
| Weekend | Story content only (class recap, weekend vibe) | Whoever is in-studio |

### How Organic Content Supports Paid Campaigns

> "When someone sees an ad and clicks through, the first thing they do is check the Instagram page. If the page is full of trendy reels with no substance, they bounce. If it's full of real people having real results in a real studio, they convert. The organic feed IS the trust layer for your paid campaigns."

### When the Business Partner Arrives (mid-June)

- He becomes the face of content. Quick, authentic clips:
  - "Hey, I'm [name], one of the owners. This is our studio in Geelong..."
  - Walk-and-talk through the studio, introduce the team
  - Event lead-up content: "We've got something coming at the end of June..."
- Not scripted. Not polished. Genuine.
- This is the piece that's been missing — a person who represents the brand on the ground.
